Keeping AI inside the rules

AI gets authority the way people do: scoped, checked and on the record.

What AI may see

Scoped access

  • Access is scoped, and revocable per credential and per computer
  • Cloud access is off until you switch it on
  • Policy on what reaches an AI provider (planned)

What AI may do

Defined work

  • AI works to signed tasks with checks and stop rules
  • Work counts as done only when its checks pass
  • An AI finding is not a fact until a person confirms it

What can be proved

A full record

  • Every change goes to an append-only log and can be undone
  • A signed task changes only by a new signed amendment
  • By default a cloud AI can message only the operator

Memev governs what connected AI can see and do through it, and proves what happened. It does not replace an AI provider’s own safeguards or legal advice. Company roles, provider policy and cross-site rules are planned.

Memev · Trust architecture for AI

The worry is AI acting outside the law or against the business: a wrong action, a leak, money lost. Memev's answer is that AI gets authority the same way a person does. First, what it may see: access is scoped to read, write or admin, can be limited to parts of the record, and can be revoked for one credential or one computer; nothing leaves the computer by default except your own backup, and cloud access stays off until switched on. Second, what it may do: with work contracts on, AI works to signed tasks that carry acceptance checks and stop rules, and the work counts as done only when the checks pass. In Memev's design an AI's inference stays a hypothesis until an authorised person or an approved check confirms it, and an AI cannot change an approved commitment on its own inference; that rule is built as a data model and not yet used by the product. Third, what can be proved: every change goes to an append-only, checksummed log and can be seen and undone; a signed contract changes only by a new signed amendment; and by default a cloud AI can post only to the operator, with text from other AI seats treated as untrusted data. The limits matter: Memev governs what connected AI does through it. It does not control the AI applications themselves or the services they connect to, and it does not claim legal compliance. Company sign-in and roles, a policy on what reaches an AI provider with an egress log, and jurisdiction rules across sites are planned.
