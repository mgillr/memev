### Added

- The memory graph can hold a finding: a claim with its source events and versions, the entities and owners it affects, a scope, a time context and a confidence. Evidence alone takes a finding no further than theory; fact needs a person's adjudication or a passing verification record, and ordinary claims keep today's rule. Dismissing, correcting, adjudicating and verifying a finding are logged operations, a dismissed finding does not come back on the same sources and versions, and a reader only sees the sources their scope allows. Nothing raises a finding yet; `docs/ORGANISATIONAL-INTELLIGENCE.md` describes the model and marks the layer as planned.
- A finding's operations are made from a credential: `Graph.readerFor` resolves a verified token or role binding into a reader that only the graph makes, and `bindFinding` and `bindEvent` set `by` from it and ignore any value the caller passes. A token with no identity scope acts as the agent it names, never as the owner person. A verification must name one of the finding's own source events, and it never replaces a person's decision. A decision over the evidence says so in `overridden`. A partial view fails closed (no id, status, confidence or context), a dismissal holds only in its scope and its refusal does not name the finding, and a listing's filters do not give away what a partial view withholds. Who may adjudicate, verify, correct and dismiss, the confirm and reject outcomes and `overridden` are provisional (ADR 5, SO-A-05, PG-10.1). Nothing calls the entry points yet, so the guarantee holds only for callers that use them.
