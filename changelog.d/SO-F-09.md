### Added

- The memory graph can hold a finding: a claim with its source events and versions, the entities and owners it affects, a scope, a time context and a confidence. Evidence alone takes a finding no further than theory; fact needs a person's adjudication or a passing verification record, and ordinary claims keep today's rule. Dismissing, correcting, adjudicating and verifying a finding are logged operations, a dismissed finding does not come back on the same sources and versions, and a reader only sees the sources their scope allows. Nothing raises a finding yet; `docs/ORGANISATIONAL-INTELLIGENCE.md` describes the model and marks the layer as planned.
- A finding's operations are made from a credential: `resolveActor` (in `identity.mjs`) resolves a verified token or role binding to an actor, and `Graph.bindFindingEvent` sets `by` from it and ignores any value the caller passes; the same actor is the reader a view or listing takes. A token of the personal scope that names an agent is that agent, never the owner person, and `resolveRequest` is unchanged. A verification must name one of the finding's own source events, and it never replaces a person's decision. A decision over the evidence says so in `overridden`. A partial view fails closed (no id, status, confidence or context), a dismissal holds only in its scope and its refusal does not name the finding, and a listing's filters do not give away what a partial view withholds. Who may adjudicate, verify and dismiss, and the confirm and reject outcomes, are one frozen table, `FINDING_AUTHORITY`, provisional until ADR 5 and SO-A-05 are decided; only a person or an operator may dismiss. Nothing calls the entry point yet, so the guarantee holds only for callers that use it. `bindFindingEvent` copies its data as plain data before it checks it, and a verification is checked against the source events the actor may read, so the refusal does not tell a withheld event from a missing one. Raw `applyOp` and replay still trust `by`, and that must close before any writer calls the entry point.
